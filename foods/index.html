Why is my dog so itchy?
Fleas, environmental allergy, food allergy and skin infections cause most itch in dogs. Where and when it shows up helps your vet tell them apart.

Why does my dog keep licking their paws?
Daily paw licking is one of the most common signs of itch in dogs. The usual causes, one paw versus all four, and what to tell your vet.

Cat overgrooming and bald patches: is it itch?
Itchy cats rarely scratch in front of us; they overgroom. What it looks like, the usual causes (fleas, food, environment) and how a diet trial works in cats.

Food allergy or environmental allergy? How to tell them apart
Both make dogs and cats itchy in the same places. The clues that point one way or the other, why allergy tests do not settle it, and what does.
📏
Scoring itch the same way every day
A steady 0–10 score beats a perfect one. Trends over weeks matter more than any single day.
🏷️
Reading a pet food label
"Chicken dinner" and "with chicken" mean very different amounts. Check every protein in the list.
🍽️
Switching foods without an upset stomach
Mix old and new over 7 to 10 days, and slow down if the stool gets soft.
🚨
If your pet ate something toxic
Act fast, but do not make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you to.
🐾
Flea allergy: one bite is enough
An allergy to flea saliva makes pets itch even when you never see a flea. It is the first thing vets rule out.
🌞
Pollen, dust mites and seasonal itch
Environmental allergy usually starts young and follows the pollen seasons. A daily itch score shows your vet when it rises.
👂
Itchy ears and ear infections
Head shaking, scratching and a bad smell are common signs. Allergy is a frequent root cause, so ears often flare with the skin.
🎥
Filming itch for your vet
Pets often act differently in the clinic. A short, dated clip from home shows what a description can’t.
