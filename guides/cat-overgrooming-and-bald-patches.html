Cat overgrooming and bald patches: is it itch?

Cats rarely scratch where we can see it. When they itch, they groom: more often, harder, and in the same spot until the fur thins. Many owners notice the bald patch long before they notice the behavior.

What itch looks like in cats

  • Short, broken or missing fur on the belly, inner thighs, flanks or forelegs
  • Small crusty bumps along the neck and back, easier to feel than to see
  • Scratching at the head, neck and ears, sometimes with scabs
  • Grooming bouts that are longer or more frequent than before, often out of sight

The usual causes

Fleas come first. Cats are thorough groomers and remove fleas before you see them, so a cat with flea allergy may show no fleas at all. In cats the itch can sit on the head and neck as well as the back, tail base and groin. Every pet in the house needs prevention, including indoor cats.

Food allergy is next, most often to beef, fish or chicken, and often shows as itch around the head and neck. Environmental allergy to pollen, dust mites or molds causes the same overgrooming patterns. Less often the cause is ringworm, mites or pain, for example in the bladder, which can make a cat lick its lower belly.

"It's just stress" comes last

Stress-related overgrooming does exist, but vets usually treat it as a conclusion reached after fleas, food and other itch causes have been ruled out. A cat whose fur loss is put down to stress before those checks may still simply be itchy. If you hear "stress" before a flea trial and a diet trial, it is fair to ask about them.

A diet trial in cats

The plan is the same as in dogs: about 8 weeks on a diet your vet chooses, nothing else, then old foods back one at a time. Re-introduced foods usually cause a flare within a week in cats.

Cats make it harder in their own ways: they can be picky, may hunt outdoors and love other pets' bowls. Switch gradually and never let a cat go without food: a cat that refuses to eat for more than a day needs a call to the vet.

What to keep track of

  • Where the fur is thin, with a photo every week in the same light
  • Grooming bouts you notice, and hair in vomit or more hairballs than usual
  • The dates of flea prevention for every pet in the house
  • Everything your cat eats, including treats, and anything caught outdoors

Frequently asked questions

Why is my cat licking its fur off?

Usually because it itches. Fleas, food allergy and environmental allergy are the most common causes. Stress grooming exists but is normally considered only after those are ruled out.

Can an indoor cat have fleas?

Yes. Fleas come in on other pets, people and clothes. Allergic cats groom them away, so you may never see one.

What foods are cats most often allergic to?

Beef, fish and chicken are the proteins most often involved in cats.
