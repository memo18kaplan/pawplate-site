Why does my dog keep licking their paws?

Paw licking is one of the most common signs of itch in dogs, and one of the easiest to miss because it looks like grooming. A quick lick after a walk is normal. Licking every day, or licking that leaves the fur stained and the skin red, is worth finding out about.

Licking is itch too

Itch is more than scratching. Licking, chewing, nibbling and rubbing all count, and owners often do not read them as itch. Signs that it has been going on for a while: red-brown staining between the toes from saliva, red or swollen skin, a yeasty smell and dogs that wake up at night to lick.

One paw or all four?

Sudden licking of a single paw, especially with limping, points to something local: a cut, a broken nail, a splinter or a grass seed between the toes. Check the paw in good light and see your vet if it is swollen or painful.

Licking of several paws that goes on for weeks points more towards allergy. Paws are one of the classic places for both environmental and food allergy, often together with the ears, face and belly.

The usual causes

  • Environmental allergy: pollen, grasses and dust mites, often worse after walks or in certain months
  • Food allergy: year-round licking, often with ear trouble or soft stools
  • Yeast or bacterial infection between the toes, which often follows the licking and keeps it going
  • Something stuck in or injuring the paw (usually one paw, sudden)
  • Less often: joint pain, boredom or anxiety, which your vet considers once itch causes are ruled out

What helps in the meantime

Ask your vet whether rinsing or wiping the paws after walks makes sense for your dog; it can reduce the pollen left on the skin. Dry well between the toes afterwards. Do not use human creams, tea tree oil or vinegar on the paws, and only use a cone or socks if your vet suggests them.

Track it for a couple of weeks

Paw licking is easy to underestimate when you only see some of it. Give the itch a 0 to 10 score each day, note whether it was worse after walks or meals, and film a licking episode: a nervous dog often sits perfectly still at the clinic. Two or three weeks of notes tell your vet far more than a single visit can.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for dogs to lick their paws?

Brief licking to clean up after a walk is normal. Licking every day, licking that stains the fur or makes the skin red, or licking that wakes your dog at night usually means itch or pain.

Can food allergy make a dog lick their paws?

Yes. Paws are one of the most common places for food allergy to show up in dogs. An elimination diet, done with your vet, is the way to check.

When should I take my dog to the vet for paw licking?

Straight away for limping, swelling, bleeding or a bad smell. Otherwise, book a visit if daily licking goes on for more than a week or two.
